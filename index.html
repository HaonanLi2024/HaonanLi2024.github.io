    About

    I study how environmental exposures alter microbial and metabolic processes to shape neurological and cardiometabolic health.

    I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Environmental Health and Engineering at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, working with Associate Professor Tanya L. Alderete in the ECLIPSE Lab. My research integrates environmental epidemiology with multi-omics, including signaling lipidomics, metabolomics, and gut microbiome profiling, to investigate how environmental exposures shape human health in longitudinal cohorts.

    My path into environmental health began with a fascination for living systems. At Xiamen University, I trained in marine biology and studied extracellular vesicles released by marine bacteria. At Peking University, under the mentorship of Professor Tong Zhu, I developed LC-MS/MS methods to quantify low-abundance signaling lipids and applied them in longitudinal panel studies of middle-aged and older adults to investigate how air pollution and ambient temperature alter pathways involved in inflammation, thrombosis, and atherosclerosis. This training spanned the full environmental health research process, from designing field panel studies and monitoring air pollution to processing biospecimens, operating LC-MS instruments, and conducting statistical analyses.

    At Johns Hopkins, I have expanded my research to birth cohorts, examining air pollution constituents and sources, per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), and the broader chemical exposome in relation to human milk composition, metabolomic profiles, the infant gut microbiome, and neurodevelopment. I contribute to several studies funded by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and am a member of the Environmental influences on Child Health Outcomes (ECHO) Data Analysis Center and the Johns Hopkins NIEHS P30 CHARMED Center.

    Training

    From field sampling to population-scale omics

    Peking University 2018–2023

    My doctoral program was housed in the College of Environmental Sciences and Engineering, where the program later established China's first Department of Environmental Health. Training there spanned exposure science, environmental epidemiology, toxicology, instrumental analysis, mass spectrometry, and biostatistics, and I applied each of them in the field and at the bench.

    Johns Hopkins University 2024–present

    At Johns Hopkins, my training has extended from the bench to population-scale multi-omics in birth cohorts. I analyze shotgun metagenomic, fecal metabolomic, and human milk metabolome and oligosaccharide data from a Southern California birth cohort and work with harmonized neurodevelopmental data across ECHO cohorts, applying mixture, mediation, and high-dimensional methods to longitudinal outcomes.

    Early-life exposures and child neurodevelopment

    Resolving prenatal PM2.5 into its chemical components to identify which constituents and sources matter for early neurodevelopment, and how gut microbial and fecal metabolic pathways may link them.

    Nickel, vanadium, copper · ship-fuel combustion and traffic · El Sendero, ECHO

    Environmental chemicals and human milk

    Characterizing PFAS in human milk as an early-life exposure pathway, and how they relate to milk metabolites, human milk oligosaccharides, and the developing infant gut microbiome.

    PFAS · HMOs · milk metabolome · infant microbiome

    Air pollution and metabolic health in youth

    Integrating PM2.5 components with serum metabolomics and lipidomics, hepatic imaging, and genotype to identify hepatotoxic components and susceptible groups.

    Liver fat and stiffness · PNPLA3 · metabolic mediators

    Signaling lipidomics for population studies

    Developing sensitive, high-throughput LC-MS/MS methods for oxylipins, sphingolipids, and lysoglycerophospholipids, and applying them to panel studies of air pollution and temperature.

    HILIC-MS/MS · online SPE-LC-MS/MS · cardiovascular pathways
